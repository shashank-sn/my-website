founder story / prototyping

how scissors and feedback built our brand.

happy beginnings got better when we stopped printing every idea and started cutting rough samples by hand.

june 25, 2025by shashank sn·5 min read

i was in my dad’s office staring at a pile of wedding invitation samples nobody wanted.

i had convinced myself that happy beginnings had to look expensive before anybody would take it seriously. every new idea became another print order. another finish. another vendor telling me the premium version would finally make the business feel premium.

the pile kept growing. sales did not.

my idea of premium was mostly ego

the invitations couples liked were rarely the ones that cost the most to produce. they liked the ones that felt like them.

that sounds painfully obvious now. it was expensive to learn then.

we were making final products before we had earned the right to finalise anything. a hunch moved from my head to a printer, then returned as hundreds of copies of the same mistake.

paper made the argument cheaper

we started building rough samples with paper, scissors, tape, and whatever was close enough to show the idea.

sometimes we cut them while watching cricket. sometimes i sent a photo on whatsapp and asked a couple what they wanted moved. a flower shifted. a line changed. somebody asked us to add their dog. we could make the change before committing money to it.

a rough thing people can react to beats a polished thing you can only defend.

feedback arrived in hours. weak ideas died before printing. couples could see themselves inside the work while it was still cheap to change.

the customer joined the process

the samples were rough, which made conversation easier. nobody had to be polite about a piece of paper held together with tape.

that changed our role too. we stopped guessing what premium meant from a desk. we watched where people paused, what they touched, and which details they wanted to show their families.

some customers disliked the unfinished process at first. once they saw how quickly their feedback appeared in the next version, the roughness made sense.

prototype the expensive belief

every business has an assumption that is quietly eating the budget.

the product needs this feature. the packaging must use this material. customers expect this page. the launch needs this campaign.

find the assumption that becomes costly once it leaves the room. build the cheapest version somebody can react to. put it in front of the people who will eventually pay for it.

  • 01show the shape of the idea before building its finish.
  • 02ask for a choice or a reaction. general feedback tells you very little.
  • 03spend only after the same signal appears more than once.

happy beginnings became clearer when we stopped trying to look finished.

the scissors gave us a way to separate what customers wanted from what i wanted them to want.
