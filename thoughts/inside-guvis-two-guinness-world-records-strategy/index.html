case study / brand strategy

inside GUVI’s two guinness world records strategy.

a first-hand account of the moves that took a vernacular edtech company from a sharp founding belief to two world-record-sized proof points.

march 20, 2026by shashank sn·12 min read

conflict of interest declaration: i was GUVI’s head of brand for the past year. i helped them win their second guinness world record. i was in the rooms where strategy was debated, pivoted, and executed.

so yes, i’m biased. but i can also tell you what happened inside those rooms.

a year ago, i was in a strategy meeting in chennai, looking at a whiteboard full of edtech logos. smiling graduates. career-transformation promises. taglines that disappeared the second you looked away.

someone asked the useful question: why would anyone choose us over the other 47 platforms teaching the same python course?

GUVI had answered that question years before i joined. that answer is the real case study.

the founding bet: what if tech spoke your language?

in 2014, arun prakash, a graduate from a tier-two college in tamil nadu, saw what the category had accepted as normal. tech education happened in english, leaving many learners outside the door.

his answer was simple: says who?

GUVI launched as india’s first vernacular tech education platform: python in tamil, web development in hindi, data science in telugu. it made existing ambition legible in the languages people already lived in.

the biggest brand opportunities sit inside assumptions everyone has stopped questioning.

the operator who made the bet work

in 2016, vasanth vijayabaskar joined GUVI as an intern. ten years later, he is the company’s chief strategy officer. the operating pattern matters more than the title.

arun set the direction. vasanth worked through the chaos, pivots, and difficult bits that turn a direction into a company. vision without an operator is a speech. this was a machine.

₹28 lakhs in 30 days

GUVI began as a b2b company: work with colleges, train students, wait months to close a deal. in january 2020, vasanth bundled ten courses, priced them at ₹2020, and took the offer directly to learners.

the media budget was whatever had come in the previous day. the stack had one job: find out whether people would pay directly.

₹28lrevenue in 30 days
50%of annual revenue at the time
₹2020the deliberately timely price

it proved the audience existed. more importantly, it gave GUVI permission to build for individuals instead of only institutions.

when the product outgrows the brand

GUVI’s coding courses had earned trust. the ui/ux course missed its audience. conversion fell and acquisition costs rose because design aspirants lacked a place in a coding brand.

the response was a ui/ux learnathon and career fair: free access, a real-world task, and a direct line to hiring partners for the best work.

30k+registrations
3×conversion from that pool
proofinstead of a new claim

brand perception moves when experience gives people something concrete to repeat. placing designers changed the perception.

the guinness gambit

by 2023, newer edtech companies were making the same vernacular claims. arun let the world establish who had been there first.

ai for india 1.0 taught python and ai in seven indian languages, in partnership with aicte, github, and nasscom. the record to beat was roughly 1,200 learners taught python in 24 hours. GUVI set a target ten times higher.

1.23llearners in 24 hours
100×the previous record
7indian languages

the event became a national story because the scale made the category position visible. the action carried the narrative.

the strongest positioning is demonstrated until the argument becomes boring.

the HCL factor

when HCL invested in GUVI, it brought more than capital. it brought proximity to a large employer’s view of skills, hiring needs, and changing technologies. that created a structural advantage.

the company could say: we hear what skills matter from one of the world’s largest tech employers. competitors can imitate an ad. building that relationship takes much longer.

the second record was a market-expansion plan

by 2024, GUVI was strong in south india. the second guinness world record for most users to take an artificial intelligence lesson in 24 hours was built for new markets and new audiences.

350k+registrations
40k+eligible learners
50m+impressions

the execution combined regional influencers, geo-targeted acquisition, and community partnerships. mini-hackathons and design challenges made the course a useful preparation step. people could try GUVI before a sales conversation.

that changed the follow-up. the sales team spoke with people who had already experienced the teaching.

what founders can steal

  1. 01find the accepted assumption. “tech must be taught in english” was an industry habit.
  2. 02prove, don’t claim. if perception needs to move, build an experience people can point at.
  3. 03build structural advantages. a real moat has to be harder to copy than a landing page.
  4. 04make marketing a product sample. let the work demonstrate the value before the pitch starts.
  5. 05let scale do your arguing. when the action is big enough, the story travels without help.

the quiet truth

brand is what you repeatedly prove, at scale, over time.

GUVI’s vernacular position grew from a decade-long commitment. the world records demonstrated capability at scale. the HCL partnership changed what GUVI could credibly say about the market.

every large brand moment followed the same order: action first, narrative second. the story wrote itself because the work was worth writing about.
