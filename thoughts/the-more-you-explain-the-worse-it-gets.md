# the more you explain, the worse it gets.

adding words is often evidence that the positioning underneath is not working.

may 7, 2026by shashank sn·3 min read

there was a glass door near the lobby of a building where i used to work.

heavy frame. large silver handle. the word “pull” printed clearly on the glass.

every day, somebody would grab the handle and push. when the door refused to move, they added a second hand, then a shoulder, and sometimes a small grunt.

the door kept asking for a different action.

## more force feels like progress

when founders struggle to explain what they do, they usually add words.

they try a sharper adjective, another paragraph, a new metaphor, a rewritten profile, and eventually a larger website with more room to clarify everything.

the explanation grows while understanding stays flat.

> every extra sentence can be evidence that the sentence underneath is missing.

## find the unresolved choice

confusing copy often begins with an unsettled business decision.

the audience is too broad. the category changes from one conversation to another. the promise combines several unrelated outcomes. nobody has chosen which problem deserves to lead.

copy cannot settle those choices by sounding better. it can only hide the disagreement for a while.

## pull before you rewrite

remove the page and answer three questions in plain language.

- 01who arrives with the problem? name a recognisable person or company.

- 02what are they unable to do? describe the stuck moment without your solution.

- 03what changes after the work? use an outcome they would notice.

once those answers are stable, the writing usually gets shorter on its own.

the door never needed more force.
