# they went with the cheaper one.

what selling specialty coffee taught me about being easier to choose.

august 10, 2026by shashank sn·4 min read

losing a deal to somebody cheaper creates a particular kind of anger.

you know how much thought went into the work. you can see the corners the other option will probably cut. the rejection makes the buyer look irrational.

i used to feel the same way when i ran el mejor coffee.

## the coffee was genuinely good

we sourced from farms we respected. the coffee was roasted after the order. the farmer’s name went on the bag. i spent an unreasonable amount of time thinking about quality.

customers who tried it liked it.

many people never reached that point. they bought from brands that were easier to recognise, easier to describe, and easier to choose quickly.

i kept reacting by improving the product through another variety, a different roast, and more information about sourcing. each improvement gave existing believers another reason to care. it did very little for a first-time buyer trying to decide between five bags of coffee.

## buyers cannot inspect everything

a buyer rarely has enough time or expertise to identify the objectively best option.

they use signals. does this feel made for me? can i explain the difference? do i understand what happens after i buy? does the choice feel safe?

price becomes powerful when every other signal is fuzzy. it is the one difference anybody can calculate.

> the cheaper option often wins after the clearer option fails to appear.

## clarity is part of product quality

good work hidden behind vague language is difficult to value.

“fresh specialty coffee” sounded fine. it also sounded like dozens of other stores. the details i cared about had to be translated into a decision the customer could make: which coffee fits their taste, why freshness changes the cup, and why ordering from us was worth the extra effort.

that translation is positioning. it turns internal quality into a difference a buyer can use.

## look at the lost deal again

when somebody says they chose the cheaper option, ask what felt easier about it.

the scope may have been obvious, the problem named more precisely, or a fear removed from the proposal. sometimes the budget was fixed and the cheaper option was the honest choice.

you need the real answer. resentment cannot improve the next decision.

quality matters after the buyer can recognise it. your job is to make that recognition happen before price becomes the only visible difference.
