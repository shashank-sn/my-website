field test / websites

the five-second test most founders fail.

your homepage can look considered and still make sense only to people who already understand you.

april 13, 2026by shashank sn·4 min read

a few years ago, i sent my website to a friend who had no background in my work and no reason to be polite.

i asked what he thought i did.

he looked for a few seconds, scrolled once, and said, “some kind of marketing thing?”

the answer was vague enough to be useless, and it was wrong.

the invisible loss

losing a deal is visible. you receive the email, feel bad for an afternoon, and eventually learn something.

confusion leaves quietly. a visitor fails to understand the page and closes it. no rejection arrives. the same thing can happen for months while the website continues to look finished.

i had spent weeks on mine. every section made sense from inside the business. familiarity had filled the gaps for me.

a clear website works for someone who has none of your context.

run the test with a human

take a screenshot of the first screen of your homepage, show it to somebody unfamiliar with the business, and remove it after five seconds.

ask four questions:

  1. 01ask them to explain what the business does in one sentence.
  2. 02ask who they believe it is meant for and listen for specificity.
  3. 03ask what seems different from the alternatives they already know.
  4. 04ask what they would do next if the problem were theirs.

ask for their first answer and stay quiet while they respond. the urge to help is part of the test.

look for the pattern

one confused person can be noise. run the test with five people and compare the words they use.

if everybody chooses a broad category such as “consulting,” “technology,” or “marketing,” the page has identified the field and missed the position.

if the audience changes in every answer, your cues are competing. if nobody sees the next action, the hierarchy needs work.

use tools after people

an image model or language model can provide more interpretations once the human tests are done. remove private customer information before uploading anything. its output is one more reading of the page and should never outrank repeated human confusion.

the purpose is to find repeated confusion. the fix may be a headline. it may also be a decision about audience, category, or offer that the page has been carrying for the business.

your website has five seconds before familiarity stops helping.
