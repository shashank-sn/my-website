product strategy

amazon gives $1b away every year.

the free thing is often only the wedge. the real product is what it lets people become.

december 15, 2025by shashank sn·8 min read

every business thinks it is selling a product. most of the time, it is selling a future.

the product is the artifact. the receipt. the thing that sits in a folder somewhere while the real value compounds in the customer’s life.

that distinction changed how i think about strategy work.

the document nobody opened

a few years ago, an edtech company hired me for brand strategy. they were about to take some huge risks and wanted the foundation in place first.

i built the positioning, the framework, and the campaign roadmap. three months later i asked the founder where the strategy document was being used.

“i don’t know,” he said. “maybe google drive?”

the engagement had worked. the risks worked. the brand moved. the document had gathered dust since the second month.

at first i felt like i had made something disposable. then i realised they were using me, rather than the deliverable. the document was the excuse. the actual product was having one sane person in the room while they took an unreasonable risk.

they were buying the possibility of confident action.

amazon’s free wedge

amazon web services gives away close to a billion dollars in cloud credits every year. the credits function as a wedge, with real value for the startup receiving them.

a startup gets infrastructure beyond its budget, builds on it, finds customers, and grows. if it becomes one of the companies people talk about, the infrastructure bill grows with it.

the free credits are the artifact. the possibility is an entire company that can exist because the first constraint was removed.

the clever part is that aws can start by making the next step possible, without asking a founder to buy everything on day one.

canva sells confidence

canva began with people who never called themselves designers. it made “i am learning design” feel possible.

the template is only the visible part. the possibility is a small business owner publishing something they are proud to show, without waiting for a specialist to become available.

the same pattern appears everywhere. a free plan, a sample, an audit, a calculator, a first call. the thing you give away is valuable only when it opens a door to a larger change.

sell the state after the work

consultants often describe the artifact because it is easier to list. strategy deck. brand book. workshop. report.

the customer is usually buying something less tidy: the confidence to make a decision, the language to get a team aligned, or the feeling that a hard problem has a shape now.

the artifact matters. it carries the thinking. the highest-value part sits above it.

ask three questions before you write the offer:

  1. 01what will the customer be able to do after this?
  2. 02what decision becomes easier?
  3. 03what possibility was unavailable before?

if the answer is only a file, the offer is still sitting at the surface.

the wedge should lead somewhere

free works when the first experience is connected to a future you can actually deliver.

an audit that leads to a vague pitch creates suspicion. a free tool that creates a problem nobody can solve creates frustration. the wedge has to be useful on its own and honest about what comes next.

the best companies show the business model clearly. the next step feels obvious because the first step created real value.

a better product question

when i review a new offer now, i start with “what becomes possible if this works?” rather than “what are we making?”

that question changes the positioning, the onboarding, the pricing, and the proof.

the strategy document can be forgotten. the confidence it creates cannot be. the cloud credits can expire. the company built with them keeps paying.

the product is the possibility.
