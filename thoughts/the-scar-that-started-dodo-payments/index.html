case study

the scar that started dodo payments.

a payments company built from the revenue its founder was unable to collect.

december 9, 2025by shashank sn·12 min read

ayush agarwal had a million users on his gaming platform, tournafest. forty percent were outside india. none of that demand could become revenue.

users in bangladesh, europe, and the middle east had cards in hand. gaming was a restricted category in several of those markets. the gateways said no. the tax and compliance work was too much for a small team.

so they gave up on forty percent of the market.

the scar became the company

in 2023 ayush teamed up with rishabh goel, who had worked in payments at wise and prodigy finance. ayush had lived the problem from the founder side. rishabh had seen it from the infrastructure side.

that combination is stronger than a market gap. a market gap is a slide. a scar is a memory of exactly where the system failed.

in early 2025 dodo payments raised a $1.1m pre-seed round from antler, 9unicorns, venture catalysts, and angels from companies including payu, oyo, flipkart, and a16z. the signal went beyond money. it was a group of people betting that the founders understood the problem from closer than anyone else.

a name that lowers the temperature

fintech companies often sound like they were named by a committee trying to reassure a bank. globaltrust payments. securecore. apex financial solutions.

dodo is an extinct bird. it is odd, memorable, and a little funny. that is useful when the customer is a solo developer who built an ai tool in their bedroom and wants to charge for it by tuesday.

the name tells the customer how the relationship might feel: less intimidating, less ceremonial, and easier to understand.

different customer, different brand energy.

specificity is a release valve

dodo chose a smaller group of merchants: digital and ai-native products and the people behind them.

that choice shows up in the messaging, the product, the partnerships, and the content. when you know exactly who you serve, decisions get smaller. when you try to serve everyone, every decision becomes a compromise.

specificity gives a small team room to be good.

talk about outcomes

payments has a jargon problem. payment rails, merchant acquiring, pci-dss compliance, settlement windows, interchange fees. the average founder rarely wakes up wanting to learn those nouns.

the useful message is the outcome: sell to customers in more countries without becoming a tax and compliance expert.

one truth can have many expressions, but the truth has to stay visible. lead with the founder’s desired future, then explain the machinery only as far as it helps.

consistency is infrastructure

many startups nail the homepage and lose the thread everywhere else. support docs sound different. the sales deck uses another vocabulary. the dashboard asks customers to do the opposite of what the marketing promised.

dodo’s brand has to travel through its messaging guide, visual library, user experience, docs, support, and social content. consistency is a system that keeps every touchpoint telling the same story.

the product is the proof

you can claim simplicity in a headline. the dashboard gets the final vote.

if onboarding takes forty-seven steps, the real brand is complicated. if documentation reads like a stereo manual, the promise is already broken.

ux is proof.

every screen can confirm the customer’s decision or make them regret it. the best brand work is often invisible because it removes one more reason to hesitate.

choose a different game from stripe

stripe, razorpay, paypal, and adyen own the obvious version of the category. a young company cannot win by becoming a slightly faster version of the same thing.

dodo reframed the job from payment processor to monetisation engine. it narrowed the customer to builders who need to go live across borders quickly.

when you are small, focus is a feature. a giant has to build for millions of merchants. dodo can build for the solo ai developer who needs to ship in forty-eight hours.

story makes complexity bearable

founders care about getting a product into people’s hands and keeping the money flowing. payment rails are only useful when they help that happen.

the simplest editorial frame is problem, stakes, speed. start with the founder’s struggle, make the cost of staying stuck clear, then show the path forward.

that is empathy made operational. the technology arrives after the reader understands why it matters.

community is a product input

any competitor with enough engineering talent can eventually build a payment feature. community, relationships, and trust take years.

partnerships create reach while community creates relevance. a young company needs both. the useful community is a group that changes what you build next, rather than a list of people to broadcast at.

dodo’s closeness to digital builders gives it better questions. the product can respond to the exact moment a solo founder gets stuck, rather than a generic merchant persona created in a workshop.

marketing that earns attention

the strongest channels in the story were founder-led content, product-led launches, and educational work. paid ads can buy a visit. they cannot buy the authority that comes from teaching someone how to solve a hard problem.

real value is slower than a campaign and more durable than one. a founder who explains the messy part of going global becomes useful before the reader is ready to buy.

a customer story is the proof

one solo ai developer had a niche product and struggled to sell globally because compliance and tax work sat outside his skill set. with dodo, he went live in less than a week and reached customers in more than twenty countries. dodo handled the payment, tax, and currency workflows around the product so he could stay focused on the product itself.

that story is stronger than a feature list because it shows the promise in motion. a customer can picture the before and after.

trust compounds

payments touches revenue, livelihoods, and businesses. trust is the main product around the main product.

dodo’s useful trust pillars are predictability, transparency, and compliance. no surprises. nothing hidden. no shortcuts that create a bigger problem later.

trust is built through hundreds of small moments where the company does what it said it would do. eventually those moments become an advantage that cannot be bought with another engineering hire.

the part worth stealing

dodo payments left “better stripe” behind and defined a different game.

the shift was from payment processor to monetisation engine, from everyone to digital and ai-native products, and from scale to agility and closeness to the founder’s problem.

the lesson is useful whether you sell software, coffee, or strategy. find the scar. choose the customer who feels it. make the experience prove the promise. then stay focused long enough for the pattern to become a brand.

### a short audit for your own company

  1. 01did you personally experience the problem, or did you only notice a market gap?
  2. 02does the name match the customer’s emotional state?
  3. 03can the mission be said in one plain sentence?
  4. 04does the homepage describe outcomes instead of machinery?
  5. 05does the product experience prove the promise?
  6. 06have you defined a game where the giants’ advantages matter less?
  7. 07are your fees, policies, and changes easy to audit?
  8. 08are you building a community, or only collecting a customer list?

dodo payments is interesting because the answers line up. the origin, name, audience, language, product, and trust system are all pointing in the same direction. that is what a brand looks like when it is built as a system.
