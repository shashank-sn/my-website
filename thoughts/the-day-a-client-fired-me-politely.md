# the day a client fired me politely.

we delivered every request on time. the client left because we had stopped bringing judgment to the work.

june 13, 2025by shashank sn·5 min read

we were four months into a retainer with a client whose logo made other people nod when i mentioned the name.

i liked that nod. it made the agency feel legitimate.

the work was moving: decks were delivered, deadlines were met, and every feedback document returned clean and polite.

the work also felt dead.

## the voice note

the client sent a voice note saying they were going in another direction.

there was no fight. they said we were doing what they asked, but they needed us to see what they could not.

that was the entire problem in one sentence.

we had become excellent at receiving instructions. i mistook agreement for service and politeness for value.

## we had removed the risky part

judgment creates friction. you have to say the requested campaign solves the wrong problem. you have to question the brief in the room where everyone else is praising it. sometimes you have to recommend less work and invoice less money.

following instructions is safer. the client cannot blame you for doing exactly what they asked.

that safety is expensive. the client already has people who can execute an instruction. they brought an outside person in because they needed another way of seeing the problem.

> if the client has to supply every important thought, you are selling hands without judgment.

## a point of view needs evidence

pushing back on everything is another form of laziness. expertise requires a better reason than disagreement.

use the customer call, the confusing product flow, the sales objection, or the numbers the team keeps avoiding. show the consequence of the current decision. propose a better one.

“this feels wrong” is an opinion. “people are misunderstanding this here, and this is the change i would make” gives the room something to examine.

## the rule i kept

after losing that client, i made a rule for my work: if i see something important, i say it while there is still time to change it.

arrive with a point of view, a reason, and a path forward. you can do that without taking over the room.

01. separate what the client requested from the outcome they need.

02. name the risk before presenting another direction.

03. put your recommendation in writing so it can be examined properly.

the polite firing hurt because the client was right. we had shipped the work and withheld the thing they were actually paying us to bring.

judgment can make a meeting uncomfortable. its absence makes the whole engagement replaceable.
